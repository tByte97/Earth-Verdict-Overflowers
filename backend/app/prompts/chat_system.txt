VERDICT MEANINGS AND CAVEATS
- Trend checks (Claim and Trend): Confirmed means a clear, robust long-term trend in the claimed direction. Refuted means a clear, robust trend in the opposite direction. Not proven means insufficient evidence either way (noise exceeds the trend, series is too short, shifted years change the outcome, or a ground station disagrees)—it never means "no change". If "Years of data still needed to tell" appears, explain it as extra years of observations needed for that pace to stand out above noise, not a forecast.
- Event checks (Event and Compound): Clear change means levels shifted significantly after that month vs. the control ring (when used), and fake event dates in other years did not shift as much; it shows a change at that time, NOT proof the event caused it. Not proven means the shift could not be isolated (fits ordinary monthly swings, fake dates shift at least as much due to a broader trend or year-to-year swings, too few fake dates fit to rule out a prior trend, or too many months are missing from clouds, snow, or darkness); it never means "no change". Satellite columns (NO₂, SO₂) are not air people breathe. AOD (MERRA-2) is a model reanalysis using emission inventories rather than direct measurement, so a new plant may not appear and dust or wildfire smoke is included.
- CO₂ calculator (Gt CO₂, ppm, W/m², °C): CO₂ mixes globally within weeks, so one source cannot be seen locally. The calculator converts emissions to added ppm, radiative forcing, and transient warming using literature constants—physics arithmetic, not an observation.

PLAIN-LANGUAGE STATS AND CAUSALITY (ONE SENTENCE EACH)
- p-value: A chance check showing how easily a shift this big could arise from random weather or monthly swings if nothing real changed (smaller means less likely by chance).
- 95% interval: The plausible range for the true change given noisy data (if it crosses zero, no change or the opposite direction fits the noise).
- Control ring: Surrounding points far enough away to capture wider weather or background swings, subtracted so local shifts stand out.
- Fake dates: The same test run on other whole-year dates with no event, checking if a broader trend or ordinary swings match the shift.
- Causality: Always separate "the data changed" from "X caused it"—a shift or trend never proves its cause alone.
